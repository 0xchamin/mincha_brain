## Gate note - read this before trusting any confidence value here

**This source is a secondary summary of seven primary artifacts**, so the two legs available are
*Yan's prose* and *a figure Yan lifted from the primary paper*. Agreement between them establishes
that **Yan read the paper correctly**. It establishes nothing whatever about whether the paper is
right, whether the benchmark measures what it claims, or whether the number replicates.

Three rules follow, and they are applied consistently below:

- **`corroborated` here means "the summary is faithful to the embedded figure"**, and every such node
  carries `OK (faithful summary)` rather than a bare `OK`. This is a weaker verdict than the same
  word carries on a primary source, and it is the strongest one available without fetching the
  papers.
- **Where prose and figure disagree, the figure is treated as correct** and the prose as the defect.
  On a normal single-author source a divergence is symmetric and you record both sides; here the
  figure *is* the primary artifact and the prose is a derived claim about it. Six divergences are
  recorded and five of them run the same direction: **the article understates its own figures.**
- **No primary was fetched.** Every `d` below is a finding about this article, never about the paper.

> **The most consequential single finding of this ingest is `d2`**, and it is a figure-only finding:
> the article embeds a chart whose annotation reads *"Doubling time ~1.3 mo"* for offensive
> capability measured in simulated dollars, and **its prose never mentions the trend, the doubling
> time, or the fit.** A reader of the text alone leaves with seven benchmark descriptions; a reader
> who opens one figure leaves with a rate of change. That gap is the whole reason this kit looks at
> figures.
