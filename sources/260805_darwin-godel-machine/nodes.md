| n1 | **The founding move: replace the Gödel machine's requirement of a *proof* that a self-modification helps with *empirical evidence* that it does.** Schmidhuber's Gödel machine rewrites itself only when it can prove the change beneficial, which "is impossible in practice" | §3, "The DGM relaxes the Gödel Machine's impractical requirement of theoretically *proving* that a change will improve the system, instead requiring *empirical evidence* from experiments to demonstrate that a proposed new version enhances performance", p4 | §1, "in practice and without restrictive assumptions about the system, it is impossible to formally prove whether a modification to an AI system will be beneficial", p2 | corroborated | OK. **The whole design follows from this substitution** |
| n2 | **Self-referential by construction: the system improves its own codebase, and that is itself a coding task**, so progress on the benchmark is progress at self-improvement | `visuals/fig1_dgm_loop.png` - the Self-modify branch takes "Coding agent's own repo + Self-improve instruction" and emits a code diff that is a "Feature to improve itself" | §3, "we define self-improvement as a coding task that involves modifying the design of an agent's own components ... the empirical evidence must reflect the system's ability to both self-improve and solve downstream tasks", p4 | corroborated | OK |
| n3 | **An archive, not a lineage: every generated agent is kept, and parents are selected from all of them**, which is what makes the search open-ended rather than hill-climbing | `visuals/fig1_dgm_loop.png` - the Archive is a *tree*, and the loop is select-from-archive, self-modify, evaluate, add-back | §3, "the DGM maintains an archive of discovered solutions during the search, facilitating open-ended exploration rather than relying on evolving a single solution", p4 | corroborated | OK |
| n4 | **Parent selection favours performance *and* under-exploration**: roughly proportional to score and **inversely proportional to the number of children already produced**, with every agent retaining non-zero probability | §3, "Parent selection is roughly proportional to each agent's performance score and inversely proportional to the number of its children with codebase-editing functionality ... ## Dropped / divergences (audit trail)

| Candidate | Verdict | Note |
|---|---|---|
| `d1` - **the evaluation is staged and noisy, and the thresholds are heuristic** | **divergence** | Agents are screened on 10 tasks, promoted to 60, and only the top two of the archive scoring over 40% reach 200 (§4.2, p6). The 40% cut is "a heuristic chosen based on the noise observed in preliminary runs". So **the headline 50.0% is measured on a different, larger task set than the numbers that decided which agents survived**, and selection pressure operated on the noisier small sets. **Finding, kept** - it is disclosed, it is a reasonable engineering compromise, and it means the search may have discarded agents that were better than their 10-task score |
| `d2` - the SWE-bench figure is on a curated subset | noted | SWE-bench **Verified** (a human-filtered, all-solvable subset) and further sampled to SWE-bench-verified-mini plus expansions. Disclosed throughout §4.2. Standard practice, and it means "50% of SWE-bench" is not the same statement as "50% of SWE-bench Verified's full set" |
| `d3` - "self-improvement" is scoped narrower than the framing implies | **divergence** | The abstract and introduction speak of a system that "iteratively modifies its own code" and could in principle "rewrite their own training scripts (including training a new foundation model)". §3 concedes "we do not show that in this paper" and that the work "focuses on improving the design of coding agents with frozen pretrained FMs". **The model does not change; the scaffolding around it does.** Finding, kept - the distinction is exactly the one [`self-improvement.md`](../../brain/topics/self-improvement.md) and [`autonomous-research-loops.md`](../../brain/topics/autonomous-research-loops.md) are separated on |
| `d4` - the meta-level is fixed, which bounds the "open-ended" claim | noted, and it is `n6` | Archive maintenance and parent selection - the search procedure itself - cannot be modified by the DGM. So the system is open-ended *within* a human-designed search, which is the same shape as S18's "the optimizer searched within a hand-designed family". Recorded because the paper's own framing (a system that "endlessly improves") reads stronger than the implementation |
| Commercial position | noted | Three authors are at **Sakana AI**, a commercial lab, which also published *The AI Scientist* - the paper S14 cites and this brain declined to ingest. No product is sold here and the code is open, so the interest is in self-improving systems being a promising direction rather than in any artifact |
| Figures 5-20, Appendices A-I | dropped | Ablation detail, prompt listings, per-modification code diffs, model-transfer results on Polyglot, and the extended safety discussion (Appendix I). Replication material. **Appendix H (using the DGM to develop countermeasures for FM hallucination) was not read** and is the most interesting un-read part |
| `github.com/jennyzzt/dgm` | not gated | **Open-sourced and not cloned.** For a paper whose entire claim is about code that rewrites itself, a docs-versus-code pass would be unusually informative - this is the best un-taken second leg of the five sources ingested today |
