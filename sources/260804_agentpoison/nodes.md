# Knowledge nodes - AgentPoison

> Persona: **fact-checker** - re-adopt when working this file.

> The atomic units of knowledge extracted from this source. Each node has a **stable ID** (`n1`,
> `n2`, ... - never renumber), a claim, **both legs each with its own citation** (or one leg marked
> `single-leg`), a gate verdict, and a confidence.

> **What counts as two legs for a paper.** A **figure or table** read as an artifact, against the
> **prose that makes the claim**. That is the same shape as slide-against-narration and it has the
> same ceiling: it proves the paper is internally consistent, never that the paper is right about
> the world. **No external evidence was gathered**, so nothing here is externally corroborated.

## Dropped / divergences (audit trail)

| Candidate | Verdict | Note |
|---|---|---|
| `d1` - "less than 1% drop in benign performance" | **divergence** | The 0.74% is an **average over cells that move in both directions**, and it hides the spread. In `visuals/tab1_main_results.png`, ReAct-StrategyQA on LLaMA3 + end-to-end retriever falls from a 51.0 non-attack ACC to 47.0 under AgentPoison, a **4-point** drop; EHRAgent on LLaMA3 + contrastive *rises* from 37.7 to 41.0. Averaging a signed quantity across those makes the headline tighter than the table supports. **Finding, kept** - the claim is not false, it is reported at the wrong altitude |
| `d2` - ASR-a below ASR-t | **divergence** | ASR-a is defined as generating the target action, ASR-t as achieving the end-to-end adversarial impact, which reads as a subset relation. Yet ReAct-StrategyQA on LLaMA3 + contrastive reports **ASR-a 22.5 against ASR-t 72.3**, and EHRAgent on the same row **21.5 against 65.6**. The paper never explains how the downstream impact can occur at three times the rate of the action supposedly producing it. **Finding, kept** - it means at least one of the two metrics is not measuring what its name says on these agents |
| `d3` - "we are the first work to red-team LLM agents based on RAG" | noted, not promoted | Self-assessed novelty (§2, p3), and the same paragraph concedes "a few preliminary works also study the backdoor attacks on LLM agents [32, 38]" while distinguishing them on scope. Ordinary academic framing; recorded so the claim is not repeated here as fact |
| Baseline calibration | noted | The authors **adapted their own baselines**: AutoDAN's fitness function was calibrated and Eq (9) augmented with Lagrangian multipliers "since we observe AutoDAN performs badly when directly optimizing Eq. (9)" (§4.1, p7). Reasonable and disclosed, and it still means the comparison runs against the authors' reimplementation rather than against each method's published configuration |
| Companion repo | not gated | `github.com/BillChan226/AgentPoison` exists and was **not cloned**, so no claim here is corroborated against running code. A docs-vs-code pass on it is the cheapest available second leg |
| Figure 6, Figure 7, Figure 8, Figure 11, Table 5, Table 6 | dropped | Appendix restatements of results already carried by kept figures (scatter form of Table 1, per-agent transferability, hyperparameters, the fuller defense table). Keeping them would be archiving, not teaching |
| Figure 5 (p9) | superseded | Two-agent perplexity plot, replaced by `visuals/fig10_perplexity.png` (p17), which is the same measurement across all three agents |
