## Read this before reading the verdicts

**Three things bound what `corroborated` can mean here, and none is visible from the table.**

**First, the two legs are one person.** The visual leg is a slide the lecturer wrote and the text leg
is the same lecturer narrating that slide. A `corroborated` verdict below means *the slide and the
speech agree* - it is a check against misreading a chart, and it is **not** evidence that the claim
is true about the world. That is the Global rules' "two legs agreeing proves internal consistency,
never truth", and it bites unusually hard on a lecture.

**Second, the presenter is senior author of three of the four papers taught.** *Large Language
Monkeys*, *How Do Large Language Monkeys Get Their Power (Laws)?* and *Archon* are all hers. Read
every efficacy number below as **self-report**.

**Third, some legs cite a slide that is not in `visuals/`.** Those were `view`ed during triage and
deliberately not kept, and they cite by **title plus timestamp** rather than file path. The slide is
real and checkable in the video; the prune rule governs storage, not evidence. See `SOURCE.md`.

## Gate summary

**31 nodes: 21 corroborated, 10 single-leg. 4 divergences, and three of them are one defect.**

`d1`, `d2` and `d3` repeat a single error: **a slide's headline is stronger than the axis beneath
it**, and each time the narration either repeats the overstatement or lets it stand. `d4` inverts the
pattern, with the *chart* honest and the *sentence* lossy.
